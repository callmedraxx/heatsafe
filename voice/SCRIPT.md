# Voice recordings for HeatSafe

HeatSafe says its reminders out loud. English and French are done. For Pidgin, Yoruba and
Hausa we need one speaker each: a calm, warm voice, like a nurse or a trusted aunt or uncle
telling you to drink, not an announcer.

## What each speaker records

**Part 1: the 9 reminder lines**, in their language. Say each one naturally, the way you
would say it to your own mother; don't translate word for word. Write down the exact words
you said for each line (we show the same words on screen).

| File name | What it means (English) |
|---|---|
| `v_water` | Time to drink water. |
| `v_cool` | Time to cool down. |
| `v_tip_shade` | Move into the shade, or indoors, for a while. |
| `v_tip_wet_face` | Wet your face, neck and wrists with cool water. |
| `v_tip_damp_cloth` | Put a damp cloth on the back of your neck. Swap it when it gets warm. |
| `v_tip_cool_wash` | Take a cool shower or bucket wash, or soak your feet. |
| `v_tip_wet_skin` | It's very hot and dry. Fans won't help on dry skin. Wet your skin first, then sit by the fan. |
| `v_target_met` | You've had today's water. Keep cooling down. |
| `v_fluid_limit` | Keep to your doctor's fluid limit. |

**Part 2: one minute of free talk** in the same language (`v_sample`): anything, e.g. what
they do on a hot day. This is for cloning their voice later, so new lines can be added
without a new session. Tell them that's what it's for and get their OK in writing (a
WhatsApp message is fine).

## How to record

- Quiet room, fan and generator off, windows closed. No echoey hall.
- Any phone voice-recorder app. Phone about 15 cm from the mouth, slightly to the side.
- One file per line, named as in the table (`v_water.m4a`, `v_cool.m4a`, …). Leave half a
  second of silence before and after.
- A normal speaking pace, a little slower than conversation. Friendly, not shouting.
- Two takes of each line is plenty. Send everything in one folder, plus the written lines.

## Who signs off

Before it ships, a second native speaker listens to the 9 lines and confirms they are
clear and natural. One person's dialect can sound odd elsewhere; the second ear catches
that.
